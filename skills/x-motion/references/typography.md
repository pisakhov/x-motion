# Typography: words are part of the composition

## Start with language

Use real copy before resolving layout. Identify the reading order and the word or phrase that carries each beat. Shorten copy only when authorized and without changing factual meaning. If required language will not fit the available time, surface the conflict instead of shrinking it until it becomes unreadable.

Treat text as both content and form. Its voice should agree with the message, not merely resemble a popular design reference.

## Design a hierarchy

Choose typefaces for their character, available weights, language coverage, and legibility at delivery size. Use licensed local font files when possible; document source and license. Confirm the loaded font rather than accepting a silent fallback.

Set roles deliberately: primary statement, explanation, label, annotation, and legal text where needed. Not every piece needs every role. Establish meaningful differences in size, weight, placement, or color; avoid making every distinction an effect.

Inspect:

- Line breaks for meaning, not just available width.
- Tracking and leading in the actual typeface and target size.
- Optical alignment rather than only bounding-box alignment.
- Numerals, punctuation, symbols, and accented or non-Latin characters.
- Contrast against the busiest background frame, not only a clean still.
- Safe areas for the intended platform, captions, and playback controls.

There is no universal font-size threshold for video readability. Canvas dimensions, scaling, viewing distance, glyph design, contrast, and exposure time all matter. Preview at the expected viewing size and report uncertainty honestly.

## Animate meaning

Choose the unit of motion to match the thought. A phrase may need to arrive together; individual letters may be appropriate when spelling, assembly, or disruption is itself the idea.

- Preserve a stable reading anchor when supporting words change.
- Use stagger only when its sequence aids comprehension or deliberate expression.
- Do not split grapheme clusters, diacritics, or shaping-dependent scripts into naïve characters. Use language-aware segmentation and test the rendered result.
- Protect kerning and shaping when wrapping words or letters in elements.
- Reserve enough settled time to read after the entrance and before the exit.
- Avoid blur, rotation, masking, or occlusion over the key phrase during its only readable interval.

Kinetic typography can be forceful without making every word compete. Decide which word earns emphasis and let the rest support it.

## Production checks

Wait for font loading before measuring or capturing. Fix composition dimensions; avoid accidental reflow caused by font swap, viewport differences, or missing assets. If measurement is needed, perform it from loaded, stable assets rather than changing layout asynchronously during capture.

Inspect the longest line, densest frame, each text transition, and the final card. Check that text is not clipped by containers, masks, or frame edges at any sampled extreme. When cards sit over footage cut from other films, sample each card at full opacity against the actual frame beneath it: a title that cleared the source film can land on the subject once the cut is retimed. Validate exact copy independently of aesthetic review.

For silent-first work, the visual track must carry the required message. For narrated work, captions should be legible and synchronized rather than treated as decorative subtitles. Do not assume automated transcription is correct without checking names, numbers, and technical terms.
