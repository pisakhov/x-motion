# Art direction: make a specific choice

## Resolve the brief

The audience's situation determines the design. Ask what they already know, what is difficult to understand, where they will watch, and what they should remember afterward. A silent mobile feed, a conference screen, and a narrated tutorial have different demands even when the brand is identical.

Distinguish:

- **Required:** truthful claims, approved copy, aspect ratio, brand obligations, legal lines, delivery format.
- **Preferred:** emotional tone, reference affinities, pacing, color tendencies.
- **Unresolved:** facts or choices that would change the concept. Clarify consequential gaps; label low-risk assumptions.

Do not turn a speculative benefit into a product claim. Do not invent logos, endorsements, or asset rights to fill a layout.

## Find the visual thesis

A useful direction links meaning to behavior. For example, an explanation of error recovery might use a continuous line that reroutes around obstacles: the line stays identifiable while its path changes. That is a mechanism for understanding, not merely a style adjective.

Write down:

1. **Message:** the one idea the piece must leave behind.
2. **Mechanism:** what the viewer sees happen that makes the idea understandable.
3. **Emotional register:** specific to the audience, not a default promise of “premium.”
4. **Evidence:** real product detail, data, copy, or imagery supporting the message.
5. **Restraint:** what you will deliberately leave out to protect clarity.

A direction can use multiple textures, scales, or scenes while retaining one thesis. “One focal point” means an intentional attention hierarchy over time, not one object forever.

## Study references without copying identity

For each reference, identify the relevant property: spatial tension, contrast, cadence, type behavior, camera distance, or sound-image relationship. Note why it helps this audience. Separate that principle from distinctive logos, illustration, layouts, signature transitions, or exact timing that should not be reproduced.

Use references as hypotheses to test against the brief. A fashionable visual language is not automatically appropriate. If references conflict, resolve the underlying intent rather than averaging their surfaces.

## Establish a visual language

Make a small set of connected decisions:

| Decision | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Composition | What is noticed first, and how does attention move next? |
| Type | What voice and reading hierarchy fit the content? |
| Color | Which roles distinguish subject, support, state, and emphasis? |
| Material / image | Does realism, abstraction, or flatness help the explanation? |
| Space | What should feel close, separate, contained, or expansive? |
| Motion | What verbs express the idea: gather, unfold, trace, interrupt, settle? |
| Sound | What should be felt or clarified, and what should remain silent? |

Avoid universal prescriptions such as a fixed font count, mandatory gradients, constant camera motion, or a transition on every cut. Use consistency where it supports comprehension; permit contrast where it communicates a change.

## Design representative frames

Use actual copy early. Establish the opening, the most information-dense beat, a major transition, and the ending. Inspect at likely viewing size, not just on a large canvas.

Check silhouette and hierarchy before surface detail. Does the subject remain identifiable at thumbnail size? Does negative space separate ideas? Is emphasis selective? Is any apparent sophistication coming only from glow, blur, or texture?

If the frame is weak while still, motion may hide it temporarily but will not repair its hierarchy. Conversely, a beautiful still may be unsuitable if its detail cannot be perceived in the allotted time. Evaluate both.

## Direction checkpoint

Before production, summarize the thesis and show the storyboard or animatic with actual evidence. Seek human direction when a choice changes meaning, tone, or brand interpretation. Do not manufacture approval from silence. When operating without a live human, proceed only with clearly labeled assumptions and reversible local work.
